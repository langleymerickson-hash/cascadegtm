How I would spend the first 90 days at Lattice

It will change with the needs of the business, my method: listen & deeply understand business problems, get the business agent-ready, then build where an agent is high leverage.

1

Learn the business, deeply

Meet with leaders and individual contributors across every function, and document the problems they are solving or know they need to solve. Then shadow people while they run their actual workflows, and document those too. You cannot fix a motion you have not watched run.

2

Run an agent-readiness audit

Audit the foundation: data architecture, availability, accuracy and ownership. Then make sure the SOPs for every core GTM motion are documented, from ICP and personas, target accounts and territories, through prospecting, the SDR to sales handoff, deal management, proposals, pricing and packaging, contracts and closing, then the sales to customer success handoff, onboarding, upsell and expansion, renewal management, and comp plans. This is the readiness work from my note on why agentic systems fail.

3

Document and rank every problem

Rank the problems by three things: revenue impact, my confidence we can solve them, and relative ease, meaning how long a solution takes to ship. To get there I listen to call recordings like they are my new favorite podcast, and I roll up my sleeves and audit all the GTM systems myself.

4

Build the roadmap with the team

Turn the audit, the interviews and the rankings into a roadmap. Get stakeholder input on both the roadmap and the prioritization method, then present it in a cross-functional meeting so the whole revenue org owns it, not just me.

5

Execute in two-week sprints

Agent readiness comes first: resolve data architecture issues, improve the data pipeline, deprecate unused fields, and sharpen the SOPs with each functional leader. Once the business is agent-ready, pick the problems where an agent is high impact, high ROI and low build time, and ship. That might be an AI twin of your buying personas, a messaging strategy engine, sales-call battlecards, or a quoting agent. I have working demos of several of these already.

Subject to change based on what the business actually needs. The sequence above is the default, not a contract.
