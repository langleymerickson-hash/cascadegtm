Contracting

Deals stall in Contracting and Negotiation. That is where we see it. It is not where it starts.

The break happens in S1 and S2. You measure it in S3 and S4.

01

Where it breaks, and why

A six-stage process. Velocity is measured in days a deal sits in a stage. Deals lose velocity or stall in S3 and S4 — but the cause is upstream.

Where the problem is created Where the problem is visible

S1 · what happens in the meeting

If the sales person is not equipped with a value based message that resonates, and a pitch deck that supports it, they need another way to keep the deal open.

So they signal flexibility. They hint at discounts, unique packaging, a new SKU, or other commercial levers — anything to close the gap that the missing value message left behind.

The commercial lever is always available. The value message has to be built. When only one of the two is ready, reps use the one they have.

S2 · what happens in the proposal

After the call the rep follows up with a proposal — often an email or a landing page that recaps the call and the use case, and includes an initial quote: products, pricing, payment terms, upfront or setup fees.

If flexibility was teased in S1, it shows up here in writing.

That plants the seed that becomes the late funnel delay. A non-standard term written into a proposal becomes a non-standard contract, which becomes legal drafting, redlines, and days in stage.

Why this is worth saying plainly

Adding legal capacity in S4 treats the symptom. The deals arriving there are non-standard because of decisions made two stages earlier, when changing them was free. Fix the input, not the queue.

02

How I would redesign it

Start by documenting the business problem and getting cross-functional sign-off on the problem, the hypothesis, and the solution. Not by building.

The problem statement, worked

Problem

New business deals are stalling in late stages. 75% of deals take 30–90 days to move through S3 and S4. Deals that stall win at half the rate of deals that clear S3 and S4 in 30 days or less. Non-standard pricing, packaging, and payment terms are common in the deals that stall.

Hypothesis

If we equip sales people with value based messaging that resonates, give them agents that assist through S1–S3, and give legal and deal desk an agent in S4, we improve deal velocity and win rate so that fewer than 10% of deals stall in S3 and S4.

Solution

Fix the foundation first — data and standard operating procedures. Then build five agents across the deal, in the order the problem is created. Then test, roll out with training, and measure against a control.

Numbers shown are the shape of the statement, not your data. The real ones come from your CRM in week one.

Prerequisites, before any build

These are not phase one. They are the condition for phase one.

  1. Audit the dataThis workflow touches Salesforce Account, Opportunity, Opportunity Contact Role, Quote, Order, Contract, Pricing and Products — plus custom objects depending on the use case. A complete audit of those objects and the data quality in key fields comes first.
  2. Write the standard operating proceduresDeal management, drafting a proposal, creating a contract, negotiating and executing it. The SOPs define what the agents enforce. Without them there is nothing to enforce.
  3. Model and approve the costAgent and platform cost modelled for current team size and volume, with approval from the manager, CFO and controller before work begins.

Then build, in this order

  1. Make sure the value message resonatesEquip sales with messaging built and pressure-tested against the buyer persona. This is the root cause, so it is the first fix.
  2. Enforce the SOPs with controls in the conversationKeep early-stage commercial discussion to standard pricing, packaging and terms, and capture what was discussed into the CRM as it happens.
  3. Make the standard proposal the fastest pathA proposal agent that makes quoting with standard terms the most efficient way to follow up after a meeting — with controls that ensure it is the only way.
  4. Build the contracting workflowSales drafts a standard contract. Executives and legal handle the rare approved non-standard case. The SOP dictates the functionality.
  5. Give legal and deal desk a negotiation agentRespond to client redlines quickly and in the interest of the business, using approved language.

Testing and rollout

Test all agents across all use cases. Roll out to individual users with training and live assistance. Preferred: hold a control group without agent access so effectiveness can be measured against a real baseline. Some businesses have no appetite for that design and prefer a full rollout measured against prior periods — that is the backup plan, and it is weaker evidence.

After rollout, four things stay funded

01Continual data audits. Other initiatives and unexpected changes break the data these workflows depend on.
02Measurement. Days in S3 and S4, percentage of deals over 30 days in those stages, and win rate by time in stage.
03A user feedback loop that drives improvements to the agents.
04Maintenance time so the workflow stays reliable and evolves with the business.
03

The agents, and where they can go wrong

Five agents plus data validation. For each one: what it is for, how it works, and what fails.

How I decide what to build

Buy

Systems of record — CRM, CPQ, ERP, e-signature, billing platforms.

Build

The workflows that connect them.

Use AI

To orchestrate people, systems and decisions — not to replace deterministic business logic.

Buyer Twin Agent

Upstream · messaging

Purpose

Draft value based messaging that resonates with the prospect.

How it works

The user collaborates with an AI twin of their core buyer persona, built from call recording data for that persona plus research. The user submits draft messaging. The twin gives direct feedback. The user revises. The loop continues until the twin approves the message. Product marketing, marketing and sales use it to develop top-of-funnel messaging and sales collateral for each persona.

What can go wrong

  • Messaging built for one level of buyer does not always resonate at another. A C-suite buyer and a manager think about the problem space and the jobs to be done differently.
  • Training on limited call data from one persona, or on calls from deals that did not close, produces messaging that will not convert.

Conversational Intelligence Agent

S1 · in the meeting

Purpose

Give the sales person live assistance on calls — surface value based messaging at key moments, keep commercial discussion to standard structures, and sync what was said back to the CRM.

Buy and build on top

AI powered conversational intelligence tools such as Gong and Clari are commodities. Do not build them. Build the workflows and automation on top of them.

How it works

  • Listens for commercial details — pricing, packaging, payment terms, discounts — and for the value based messaging.
  • Syncs that to fields on the Salesforce Opportunity, so leadership and operations can audit what value message was used and which commercials were discussed.
  • Posts the recording and the commercials discussed to a Slack channel immediately after the call, so the team can confirm in real time that we are using the right message and discussing standard deal structures.

What can go wrong

It can occasionally mishear something on the call — the number of seats, or the price per seat. The sales person reviews and edits the data before it is written to Salesforce.

Proposal Agent

S2 · proposal

Purpose

Make follow-up after the first meeting seamless and efficient, using the value based message and the standard terms actually discussed on the call.

Inputs

Call recording data from the conversational intelligence agent, updated Salesforce Opportunity and Quote data from the completed call, and a detailed standard operating procedure on quoting.

How it works

  • Appears as a landing page. The rep selects the deal, the company, the prospect, and the products, pricing and terms discussed on the call.
  • Generates a unique landing page and a follow-up email to send to the prospect.
  • The sent email syncs back to Salesforce, and automation pulls the proposal into the Slack channel for that deal so the team has visibility.

What can go wrong

Incomplete or inaccurate Salesforce data — account name, opportunity ARR, contact role title — produces inaccurate output. The foundation of this workflow is accurate data across every object it touches.

Contracting Workflow

S3 · contracting

Purpose

Let the sales team draft a standard contract, and let sales and sales leadership work together inside the SOP to produce a pre-approved contract that can go to the counterparty. In the rare case where executive management or legal has approved a non-standard format, that party uses the workflow to update the contract.

Inputs

A text template of the standard contract, and a robust SOP for contracting that includes approval workflows for packaging, pricing, payment terms and opt-out language. Plus accurate Salesforce data: Account, Opportunity, Opportunity Product, Quote Line Item, and custom objects as needed.

What can go wrong

  • Same dependency as the proposal agent — incomplete or inaccurate Salesforce data produces inaccurate contracts.
  • If the client requires the contract to be on their own paper, this workflow cannot support that as designed. It could be added in a later phase, but today it runs outside the core workflow.

Negotiation Agent

S4 · negotiation

Purpose

Assist internal legal and deal desk as they work through client redlines and draft updates to clauses and language.

Inputs

A robust SOP for negotiating a contract — which sections are editable without escalation to the executive team, examples of common edits, and frequently asked questions. Plus a dataset of approved contracts, with that language fed into the LLM powered tool.

How it works

Appears as a landing page. The user pastes the contract language in question into one field and the context for the update into another. The agent returns approved language drawn from the dataset of approved contracts.

What can go wrong

Language returned by this agent is a suggestion from an LLM, not the judgement of an experienced general counsel. Treat it as directionally correct and always verify it with a human who applies critical thinking and their own knowledge to decide whether the language serves the business and creates a mutually beneficial term.

Data Validation Automation

Foundation · all stages

Purpose

The data driving these workflows — across Opportunity, Opportunity Contact Role, Quote, Order, Contract, Pricing, Products and custom objects — must be subject to review.

How it works

  • Automation flags deals in S2, S3 and S4 that are missing the data needed to power the agent workflows.
  • An LLM powered validation workflow checks that the data is both available and accurate.
  • It produces a list of records needing review with suggested edits, which the user executes.

Cost modelling, before anything is built

Before developing any agentic solution or buying any platform, model the cost and get approval from the manager, CFO and controller for current and future use.

Agent cost is driven by token usage and price per token — tokens per run, runs per day, runs per person, number of people using the tool. Caching a share of the input reduces cost, and model choice is another lever. Platform cost is more straightforward. This recommendation uses tooling you already own — Gong, Salesforce, Slack — and the model includes those plus any updates they need.

What stays human

Final contract language is decided by counsel, always. The rep reviews and edits what the conversational intelligence agent writes to Salesforce before it is committed. Non-standard contracts are approved by executives and legal, not by an agent. AI orchestrates people, systems and decisions. It does not replace deterministic business logic.

04

What I would build first, and how I would measure it

Build in the order the problem is created, not the order it becomes visible.

First

The data audit and the SOPs. Every agent in this design fails the same way — bad data in, wrong document out. Nothing else is worth starting until the objects are audited and the procedures are written, because the SOPs are what the agents enforce.

Then the value message and the S1 controls, because that is where the problem is created. Fixing S4 first would make us faster at processing non-standard contracts we should not have generated.

How I would measure it

Success metrics
MetricWhat it tells us
Days deals spend in S3 and S4The headline velocity measure, and the one the problem statement is written against.
Percentage of deals over 30 days in S3 and S4The stall rate. Target is under 10%, from a starting point of 75%.
Win rate by time spent in S3 and S4Connects velocity to revenue. Stalled deals currently win at half the rate.
Agent adoption per userIf reps are not using it, nothing above will move. Adoption is the leading indicator.
Data health on workflow-critical fieldsThe dependency everything else rests on. Watched continuously, not once.

All of this lives in one dashboard that monitors agent usage and encourages adoption, gives visibility into the data driving the workflow, and reports the success metrics.

Where this comes from

The agents and workflows in this design are ones I have built recently — with clients at CascadeGTM and at Incredible Health. The failure modes listed under each agent are not hypothetical. They are what I have watched go wrong and had to fix.

Get the data and the procedures right, fix the message before the paper, and the late-stage queue stops forming.
